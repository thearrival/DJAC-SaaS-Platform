# 03 Regulatory Frameworks

## Coverage Strategy

DJAC should model regulations as versioned, jurisdiction-aware framework packs. Each pack can include laws, standards, controls, mappings, and local implementation guidance.

## Regional Coverage

### North America

Support U.S. and Canadian cybersecurity, privacy, sectoral, and federal frameworks including NIST, HIPAA, GLBA, CMMC, FedRAMP, PCI DSS, SOC, CIS Controls, COBIT, PIPEDA, and Canadian cybersecurity guidance.

### Europe

Support GDPR, UK GDPR, NIS2, DORA, CRA, EU AI Act, ePrivacy, PSD2, DSA, DMA, ENISA guidance, and ISO standards commonly used across Europe.

### Middle East

Support Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, and Kuwait with privacy, cybersecurity, cloud, and national assurance requirements.

### Asia-Pacific

Preserve China support and add major frameworks for Singapore, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, India, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam, and the Philippines.

### Africa

Support major privacy and cybersecurity regimes including South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt, and AU-level conventions.

### Latin America

Support Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, and Colombia privacy and data protection frameworks.

## Global Standards

Map regulations to global standards such as MITRE ATT&CK, MITRE D3FEND, OWASP, CSA CCM, CIS Benchmarks, IEC 62443, FIPS 140-3, TISAX, NERC CIP, SWIFT CSCF, FAIR, OpenCRE, OpenSSF, SLSA, SPDX, and CycloneDX.

## Canonical Framework Model

Each framework pack should support:

- metadata and versioning
- jurisdiction and language
- obligations and control statements
- applicability rules
- evidence requirements
- crosswalks to other frameworks
- AI-assisted summaries and translations
- impact analysis for regulatory changes

## Update Model

Framework packs should be maintainable as additive content so new jurisdictions can be introduced without disrupting existing comparisons or scoring.
